ASL Resources for Parents
After a Failed Newborn Hearing Test

Important: A failed newborn hearing screen is not a definitive diagnosis — many babies pass their follow-up test. However, it is smart to start learning about ASL now, regardless of the outcome. Beginning early opens a door of communication with your child no matter what the follow-up results show.

First Steps & Support Organizations

Hands & Voices

handsandvoices.org

A nationwide, parent-driven nonprofit supporting families of children who are deaf or hard of hearing. Non-biased about communication choices — supports families who sign, use spoken language, or both.

My Deaf Child

mydeafchild.org

Created by a parent of a deaf child, this site helps families get answers, access resources, and connect with a community that understands their needs.

American Society for Deaf Children (ASDC)

deafchildren.org

Offers ASL classes at beginner, intermediate, and advanced levels, plus deaf mentor programs and ASL stories for families. Resources available in English and Spanish.

National Association of the Deaf (NAD)

nad.org

If your child is identified as deaf or hard of hearing, you can request ASL instruction through your child's early intervention system — specifically designed to support families in this way.

Learn ASL — Free & Low-Cost Options

Sign It! ASL

FREE
mydeafchild.org/learn-asl

Online, self-paced ASL course for parents. Free to parents of children 36 months or younger who qualify. Covers conversational ASL, grammar, syntax, and Deaf culture.

ASL Connect — Gallaudet University

FREE
aslconnect.gallaudet.edu

Free online ASL and Deaf Studies resources from Gallaudet University — one of the world's leading universities for the Deaf community.

ASL Deafined

asldeafined.com

Subscription-based site with ASL video lessons taught entirely by Deaf professionals. Designed for parents of deaf children and anyone wishing to learn ASL.

Start ASL / Sign Language 101

FREE

start-asl.com  ·  signlanguage101.com

Free beginner ASL lessons online — great starting points before committing to a structured course. YouTube also has many free channels for beginners and parents.

ASL for Babies & Toddlers

You can start teaching your baby ASL even while you're still learning it yourself — babies can begin learning ASL as a first language. Helpful apps for early communication:

  • Smart Hands — ASL dictionary app for teaching babies and toddlers basic signs
  • Signed Stories — Videos of children's books read in sign language
  • VL2 Storybook Apps — Bilingual in ASL and English; richly visual for young children

Next Steps After a Failed Screening

3 mo

All babies who don't pass the initial screening should have a complete hearing test before 3 months of age.

6 mo

All babies confirmed with hearing loss should begin intervention services before 6 months of age.

  • EHDI-PALS — Find pediatric audiologists near you
  • CDC Parent Guide — Language, support groups, and intervention
  • My Baby's Hearing — Step-by-step guide after a failed screening
  • Your state's Early Intervention program — free for every family; ask your pediatrician for a referral. You can request ASL instruction through this program.

Children whose families provide rich language exposure at home — whether English, Spanish, ASL, or another language — are more likely to reach age-appropriate language and literacy milestones. Starting to learn even a few basic signs now puts you ahead of the curve and shows your child that you are ready to meet them wherever they are.
